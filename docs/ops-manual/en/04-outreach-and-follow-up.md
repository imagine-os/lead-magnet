# Outreach and follow-up

You are not asking for a meeting. You are telling someone that a thing exists with their name on it, and that it will be gone in fourteen days.

That is a much easier message to send, and a much easier one to receive.

## Choosing the channel

The outreach composer ships templates across six channels, in English and Spanish, with the prospect's tokens already filled in and the live page URL attached.

{{channels}}

Rules of thumb:

- **Cold email** — the default for cold prospects with a findable address. Subject does the work; the body is four sentences.
- **LinkedIn DM** — better than email for owners who post. Shorter still, and never paste a wall of text.
- **WhatsApp** — excellent in Latin America and for Spanish-first prospects. Only where the number is legitimately public or given.
- **SMS** — only warm or hot. It is an interruption, and a cold interruption is a burned contact.
- **Call** — hot and referred. The script exists to get them to open the link while you are on the phone.
- **Warm intro** — always the best channel when it exists. Ask the referrer to forward the link rather than to describe it.

## What to say

Four sentences, in this order:

1. **The fact.** "We built an operating system for Paws & Play and it is live at this link."
2. **The specific.** One detail that proves it is theirs — their city, their roles, their palette, the software they actually use.
3. **The number.** What their current stack costs per year, as a single figure.
4. **The one ask.** Open it. Not "reply if interested", not "let me know a good time". Open it.

What to leave out: our company story, a feature list, the word "solution", and any adjective about how excited we are.

The expiry is your only urgency, and it is true, so say it plainly: "it is live for fourteen days."

## The cadence

Fourteen days, three or four touches, tied to their behaviour and not to the calendar:

- **Day 0** — the link, on the best channel available.
- **Day 2–3, if they never opened** — different channel, shorter, same link. Assume the first one was never seen rather than rejected.
- **On open with no CTA click** — they looked and did not move. Send the one thing the page did not say: a single role view that matters to them ("your accountant's view is the second tab").
- **On demo open with no booking** — this is the hottest signal in the system. Offer a specific fifteen-minute slot, not an invitation to pick one.
- **Day 11–12** — the honest expiry note. "Your workspace comes down on the 30th; want me to keep it up?" Keeping it up is a reason to reply that costs them nothing.
- **After expiry** — one line, no pitch, door open.

Never more than four. If four honest, specific touches did not land, the profile was wrong or the timing was, and neither is fixed by a fifth message.

## Sending, honestly

Right now, **sending is not wired**. The composer writes a real draft as a touch row, shows you the filled template, and lets you copy it into the channel yourself; "Mark as sent" records the send so the funnel stays truthful. The send button is a visible placeholder that says it is not wired, because a button that silently does nothing is worse than no button.

Opens and clicks on the seeded data are real rows in the touches table, so the funnel maths below is exercised end to end even while the transport is manual.

How to read it without lying to yourself:

- **Sent to opened** is about the subject line and the channel. If it is low, the message never had a chance; do not rewrite the body.
- **Opened to clicked** is about the first sentence. Fix the fact, not the formatting.
- **Clicked to demo opened** is about the page above the fold. If they arrive and leave, the personalization was not visible fast enough.
- **Demo opened to booked** is about the call to action and the calendar. This is where the page is doing its job and the friction is ours.
- **Scroll depth under 30 %** on several prospects means the page is the wrong shape for that segment — that is an archetype problem, not a copy problem.

Every section view, scroll depth, CTA click, role switch and exit intent is tracked, and the engine turns the pattern into a recommendation you can accept in the composer: switch archetype, add a section, shorten, ask more questions. Recommendations are suggestions with reasons attached. Read the reason before you accept.

## Three things that lose the deal

1. **Following up faster than the value arrives.** Two touches in twenty-four hours reads as automation, and automation is exactly what we are claiming to replace.
2. **Defending a wrong guess.** If they say "we do not use that", the right answer is "good — that changes your number, here it is", not an explanation of our methodology.
3. **Sending the link without a specific.** A personalized product with a generic message reads as a mail merge, and then nobody clicks.
