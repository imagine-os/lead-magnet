# Walkthrough calls and the proposal

They booked because they saw something that was already theirs. The call is not a demo — they have already had the demo. The call is where you find out what is true about their business and decide together whether this is real.

Fifteen minutes. Keep it to fifteen. Ending early with a next step beats running long with a maybe.

[screenshot: R-01 — The proposal: the whole stack, priced, printable]

## Before the call (five minutes)

- Open their analytics timeline. Know which sections they read, how far they scrolled, which role views they switched to, and whether they came back a second time.
- Open their demo in another tab, on the role they spent longest in.
- Re-read their stack. Know which tools are confirmed and which are still guesses.
- Have the proposal open but not shared.

If they spent four minutes in the money view, this is a cost conversation. If they switched to the life view, this is a "give me my evenings back" conversation. Those are different calls.

## The fifteen minutes

**0:00–1:30 — Their words, not yours.** "You spent a while in the front desk view — what happens at your front desk today?" Ask, then be quiet. Everything after this is shaped by the answer.

**1:30–4:00 — Their screen, their data.** Share the demo, not slides. Start in the role they cared about. Move through one real task end to end: a booking arrives, it lands on a schedule, someone is notified, the money shows up in the owner's view. One thread, not a tour of features.

**4:00–6:00 — The life view.** This is the moment the call turns. "This is your partner's view. This is what your accountant sees without calling you." Nobody expects it, and it is the difference between software and an operating system for their life.

**6:00–8:30 — The stack, out loud.** Walk the list of tools and prices. Ask them to correct it. Every correction makes the number more theirs, and you should visibly welcome being wrong. Land on: what they pay now, what we cost, the difference per year.

**8:30–11:00 — What is real and what is not.** Say it plainly: what runs today, what is mocked, what we would build first for them. Nobody has ever lost a deal by being precise about this, and plenty have been lost by being vague.

**11:00–13:00 — The proposal.** Open it and scroll it with them. Do not read it aloud. Stop on the scope and the price and let the silence do the work.

**13:00–15:00 — One next step.** Name it, date it, and say who does what. Then end the call.

## The five objections

**"How much of this actually exists?"** — The honest answer, immediately. The engine, the pages, the demo, the booking, the analytics and the design system are real. Identity, the shared database, image generation, AI enrichment, real sending and payments are not, and here is the order we would wire them for you. Then show a placeholder in the product itself: we label what is not wired rather than hiding it.

**"We already have most of this."** — Agree, then reframe. They have most of the functions across eight products that do not talk to each other. Show the stack list and the total. The pitch is not new features, it is one system and one bill.

**"It is too expensive."** — Do not discount. Put our monthly price next to their current monthly spend from the audit. If it is genuinely close, the honest answer is that the win is consolidation and time, not money, and say so.

**"Our team will never adopt it."** — The strongest answer is the role views: nobody gets a general-purpose tool, everybody gets the three things their role does. Offer to start with the one role that hurts most, in one location.

**"Send me something and I will think about it."** — Print the proposal and send it during the call, then name the date the workspace expires and ask whether to keep it up. Keeping it up is a small yes, and it keeps the thread alive without pressure.

## The proposal

The proposal covers the whole stack, priced from their own profile: the website and purchase flow, the customer app, the staff and admin dashboards, the documentation, the operations manual, and what it replaces. It is a route, not a file — it is themed to their brand, it is bilingual, and it prints.

To print: open the proposal and use the print button. The print stylesheet drops the app chrome, opens every collapsed section, and prints the page in their palette on white. Save as PDF and attach it if they want a file, but send the link first, because the link stays current and a PDF does not.

## After the call, same day

- Write what they corrected back into the profile. Their words, not your paraphrase.
- Update the stack rows they confirmed or rejected; the number changes and that is fine.
- Record the next step where the next person can see it.
- If they asked for something the product does not do, it goes in as feedback from the product itself, not into your notes. Use the annotate button, say who asked and why, and it lands in the inbox where an agent triages it.

## What a good call looks like afterwards

The profile has changed. The savings number is different from the one you opened with. There is a dated next step with a name on it. And the prospect has said, in their own words, what they would want built first — which is the specification for everything that follows.
